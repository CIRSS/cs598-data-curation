## Course Description

Data curation, the management of data in support of analysis and use, is a critical activity across industry, science, and public policy. Data curation is the set of practices that make data discoverable, accessible, intelligible, accountable, and reusable over time, spanning the complete lifecycle from acquisition through downstream use, reuse, and disposal. This course takes a broad view of curation as a practice that supports reliability, transparency, reproducibility, and ethical use, providing a survey of the technical infrastructure and organizational judgment curation requires at scale.

This course is not primarily concerned with learning engineering or analytic techniques and instead focuses on the many other aspects of curation in the data lifecycle, training data engineers, data stewards, and technically oriented data managers to bring curatorial judgment to engineering and systems design that complements the technical skills built in other courses.


## Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

* Steward data and associated artifacts responsibly across their full lifecycle, from creation through reuse or disposal, within relevant legal, ethical, and organizational constraints.
* Evaluate data and associated artifacts for fitness for independent reuse and verifiability, assessing quality, completeness, and provenance at both the data and metadata levels.
* Make informed decisions in data curation contexts, navigating conflicting obligations and stakeholder needs under real-world constraints.
* Broker effectively across stakeholder groups, translating between data producers, technical staff, and institutional stakeholders while recognizing the limits of one's own expertise.
* Design and manage the technical infrastructure, such as metadata catalogs, pipelines, and automated workflows, that enables data curation at scale.
* Engineer reproducibility and provenance into analytical systems using version control, containerization, and pipeline lineage methods.
* Operate responsibly within large-scale analytical and ML/AI pipelines, applying curatorial principles to training data documentation.


## Prerequisites

* Completion of an introductory databases course or related experience including database design, schemas, constraints, and queries.
* Ability to create and manipulate commonly used data formats including JSON and XML.
* Basic Python programming skills including familiarity with Pandas, use of Python libraries, and web-based APIs.

### Course Components

The course is comprised of the following elements:

#### Lecture Videos
For each module, the concepts you need to know will be presented through a collection of short video lectures. The videos usually total 1.5 to 2 hours for each module. You generally should spend at least the same amount of time digesting content in the video.

#### Readings
Readings are included in each module to supplement and reinforce lecture materials. Concepts from required readings are included in module quizzes. Supplemental readings are optional and provided for students who want to explore a particular area in more depth.

#### Quizzes
Each module concludes with a graded quiz to help ensure you understood that module's content. 

#### Assignments
There are five assignments for you to complete in this course. Assignments are delivered via the PrairieLearn system.

#### Exams
Midterm and final exams.

#### Labs and Activities
Modules include one or more ungraded labs or activities. These are designed to be formative as well as to provide scaffolding for assignments.

#### Reflections
Modules include ungraded reflection activities to give students the opportunity to connect course concepts to their own interests or experience.

#### Participation
Student participation will be based on engagement via Campuswire and office hours. 

#### Extra Credit
At least two extra credit opportunities will be available worth approximately 2% of the final grade. This should be sufficient to help students on grade boundaries.

### Late Assignment Policy

* Unless otherwise specified, all assignments are due at 11:59 PM US Central Time on the due date. We encourage everyone to finish the assignments before the deadline.
* All auto-graded assessments will receive an automatic 5% penalty per day late, for up to 10 days after the due date. After 10 days no points will be awarded.
* Students may avoid the late penalty entirely by requesting approval from course staff via Campuswire in advance of the due date. Assignments submitted under an approved extension are not subject to the late penalty.
* No assignments will be accepted after the last day of class.
* For all assignment deadlines, please refer to the Course Assignment Deadlines, Late Policy, and Academic Calendar page in Coursera.
* For course extensions or incompletes, please email mcs-support@illinois.edu.

The late penalty above applies to quizzes and assignments only. Exams are administered during a fixed one-week window and are not eligible for late submission or the late penalty described above. Students who are unable to complete an exam during the scheduled window due to a documented emergency should contact course staff as soon as possible.
